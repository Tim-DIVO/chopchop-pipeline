Filters

Negative = the cut falls outside the exon, in the intron. At 0 those are hidden.
Cas9 reads the two Gs of NGG through Arg1333/Arg1335 (Anders 2014), so a SNP there costs you the cut. The N is not read. Inside the protospacer the 10 nt nearest the PAM (the seed) are the strict ones; the PAM-distal 10 are largely tolerated (Hsu 2013).
Percent identity to the human ortholog in an 11-residue window around the cut site. At 0 the filter is off; guides with no alignment there are always kept.
A frameshift upstream of an in-frame ATG can be bypassed by re-initiation, giving a shortened but partly functional protein. This is why Kroll's exon list ends with “not exon 1”. A guide is only flagged when the ATG is within 40 codons and a product re-initiated there would keep ≥70% of the protein — distance alone flags about half of all cut sites by chance and means nothing on its own.
asym = asymmetric (preferred) · sym = symmetric · faded = no guides cut in that exon

Ranking weights

Each factor is min–max normalised across the guides currently shown, multiplied by its weight, and summed. Set a weight to 0 to ignore it. Sort by Score to use this ranking.

SNP position penalty

These two are multiplicative, not additive: a SNP in a PAM G does not make a guide slightly worse, it means the guide probably does not cut at all, and no amount of efficiency makes up for that. The score is multiplied by 0.05w/3 for a PAM-G SNP and 0.5w/3 for a seed SNP, so at the default weights a PAM-G hit costs 95% of the score and a seed hit 29%. Set a weight to 0 to switch that penalty off. The penalty follows the strain you picked above: a SNP that is absent from your line is not counted.

Thresholds used by Kroll

Efficiency > 0.60 · frameshift > 80% (66% = random, below that is worse than chance) · MM0–MM3 all zero, but a single MM0 hit on an alt contig is an assembly ghost copy and can be ignored · no SNPs across the 23-mer in your strain. He gives no numeric rule for distance from the splice boundary — that filter is yours to set.
